# Yumi Omori's personal website

A two-line Personal / Research entrance, a research page, and a chronological personal blog, built for GitHub Pages. All articles are real pages, readable without JavaScript.

Every page shares one design (`public/assets/site.css`): white paper, ink text (`#20201e`) and one blue (`#284ed1`) for anything you can press. The type stays small and quiet. JetBrains Mono is used for the interface and the text of every page (including the blog list and the travel guide); Source Serif 4 only for the articles themselves. Cabinet Grotesk appears only in the punchline of the Personal detour. On a desktop the pointer everywhere is the cover's larger pixel arrow (touch screens keep the normal cursor). Links inside text (Personal, travel, reading, games) are bold blue words that pop out with a light blue shadow when pointed at; longer names tilt less, and very long ones only lift so they can wrap (the build adds `is-mid` / `is-long`). Links inside articles keep their underline. Articles are set at 18px (17px on phones) in a centered 660px column, and their images widen to 860px. A few small game touches sit on top: blog months are save points, the 404 page is a game-over screen with a Respawn button, articles have a reading-progress bar and end in a game menu (a pixel hand points at Previous post, Next post or Back to blog; ↑ / ↓ move it), and there is one hidden cheat code. Everything works with a mouse, touch or Tab; the keyboard shortcuts are optional and listed under **controls** (press `?`). Animations stop when the visitor prefers reduced motion.

## Add a blog post

Create **one Markdown file** in `content/posts/`, for example `your-post.md`:

```markdown
---
title: "Your post title"
date: "2026-09-30"
excerpt: "Your authored excerpt."
featured: true
cover: "assets/my-cover.jpg"
---

Write your article here in Markdown.
```

Commit it to `main` and it appears automatically. The filename becomes the address, and the date places it in the timeline. No separate index needs editing. The preview, reading page, previous/next navigation, and sitemap update together. Use `excerpt` for the full authored summary; the build does not truncate it or invent a summary when it is missing. Each published entry should have a `cover`, either a path in `public/assets/` or an absolute image URL. Set `featured` to `false` to exclude a draft from the build.

## Entrance and research page

The cover at `/` shows only Personal and Research, set in letter-spaced JetBrains Mono, like a game title screen: on a desktop the pointer is a slightly larger pixel arrow (the same one is used on every page), and a pixel hand jumps to the word you hover or pick with ↑ / ↓ (or j / k) and Enter. Touch screens keep the normal cursor. Research goes to `/research/`. Personal opens `/personal/hello/`: a playful Bayesian detour with oversized Cabinet Grotesk typography and a confirmation conversation.

The first time, the conversation gives ten seconds to decide between "Yes, I AM SURE" (→ `/personal/`) and "No, I just want to check on a potential PhD applicant" (→ `/research/`). At zero it says "Fine, 5 bonus seconds, as a treat.", and the No reply becomes "No, I just want to check on this PhD applicant full of potential, who I’d absolutely love to work with". When those five seconds run out too, it takes the visitor to `/research/`. The countdown pauses in a hidden tab, and closing the window or answering stops everything.

The second time the visitor presses "go to Yumi's personal" (after any ending of the first conversation), there is no question and no clock: "Wow, it’s you again.", "I guess you really tried to get into Yumi’s Personal.", "Okay, I’ll give you a free pass.", and then `/personal/` opens. From then on the research-side Personal links (the cover, the research page's navigation and the "go to Yumi's personal" link at the end of the research page) and the detour's own link go straight to `/personal/`. This state lives in `window.name` (this tab only; nothing is stored on the device or sent anywhere). Links inside the personal pages (blog, travel, reading, games) always go directly. Without JavaScript, the personal link on the detour goes straight to `/personal/`.

Edit the entrance copy (including both No wordings and the second-visit messages) in `scripts/entrance.mjs`, its extra styles in `public/assets/entrance.css`, and dialog behavior in `public/assets/entrance.js`. The entrance pages also load the shared `site.css` and `site.js` (free pass, menu pointer, controls). The pixel cursor (`assets/cursor.png`, `assets/cursor@2x.png`), the pixel hand (`assets/pixel-hand.svg`) and the arrow over the Personal words (`assets/pixel-down.svg`) are drawn in `scripts/pixel-art.mjs` and generated by the build. The detour is excluded from the sitemap and marked `noindex`. Existing personal, travel, reading and article URLs are unchanged. The wordmark and article author link to `/`, the cover.

To edit research content, update `content/site.json`. The profile image uses your GitHub avatar URL. Add papers to `content/papers.json` using `title`, `authors`, `venue`, and optional `url` fields; they are listed right under "Here are my works:". The initial papers list is intentionally empty; until it has entries, "Here are my works:" ends in a blinking cursor.

## Personal pages

`/personal/` opens with two short sentences in the same small type as the rest of the page: "I travel, read, write a lot. I also played a lot of video games." and "Below is a subset of the things I write." The blue words are links (travel → `/travel/`, read → `/read/`, write → the list below, played → `/games/`). Pointing at one, or reaching it with Tab, opens a small game window above it that types one line, with a pixel arrow pointing at the word: "I have visited 138 places in 45 countries.", "554 books in the inventory.", "Well, here I have a subset of the things I write." and "A small subset of games I play D:". The lines are the `sayings` in `scripts/build.mjs`; the travel numbers are read from `content/travel.html` and the book count from the reading list. Touch screens go straight to the page. The writing list below shows every post with its date, title and full excerpt, newest first, and the whole entry is the link. Every personal page (writing, reading, travel, games) has the same small tab row under the header.

The travel page starts with the plain sentence at the top of `content/travel.html` ("I’ve visited 138 places in 45 countries."); edit the numbers there. The games page is the `games` list in `scripts/build.mjs`: a title and one line for each game (the line may contain a link).

## Dino arena (research page)

The research page has a Dino game as wide as the text above it. The top row is the visitor's (Space or ↑ to jump, ↓ to duck, or tap and swipe down on a phone; the keys only work while the game has focus). Underneath, a Double DQN agent learns the same game from scratch in a Web Worker on the visitor's device, and every attempt gets its own row: when the agent crashes, that row stays frozen on the crash with its score, and the next attempt starts in a new row below, so twelve attempts are twelve rows. Training starts when the arena scrolls into view (with reduced motion, it waits for the Start training button), pauses when the arena is off screen or the tab is hidden, and stops once an attempt reaches 1,000 points; that row is marked solved, and Train again starts over. The line next to "Dino arena" shows the training steps and ε, and after the solve, the attempt, steps and time it took. Under the buttons, **Score box** opens a list of every attempt and its score (one block per 100 points; the running attempt is last, the solved one is blue), and **How it learns?** asks "Are you familiar with RL?": Yes gives a one-line answer, No opens a short introduction to RL and DQN with the math typeset by KaTeX (loaded from the CDN the first time; the TeX stays readable if it cannot load). Nothing is sent anywhere or stored on the device.

The game rules and the learner (a small network, replay buffer, Adam) are in `public/assets/dino-core.js`, which both the page and the worker use; drawing, controls, the attempt rows and the score box are in `public/assets/dino.js`; the markup (including the How it learns? text) is in `scripts/arena.mjs`. The hyperparameters are the `DEFAULTS` in `dino-core.js`. In a test browser, `window.render_game_to_text()` returns the current state and `window.advanceTime(ms)` steps the game. Without JavaScript the arena is hidden and the rest of the page is unchanged.

## Edit the reading list

Add book metadata to `content/books.json`. Each entry has a stable work `id`, `title`, `author`, `language`, `category`, and `year`. For a Japanese or Chinese original, keep its original title and author, and add `englishTitle` and optionally `englishAuthor`. English originals use their original English title and author, even when the downloaded edition was translated. A confirmed title with an unconfirmed author may use `author: null` and `metadataStatus: "author-unconfirmed"`; do not guess the author or edition.

The `year` is the earliest known download year (use `null` when there is no supported year). Repeated work IDs are grouped under the earliest year automatically. Download years do not establish reading completion. Years appear newest first, with Science first in every year, followed by the ordered themes in `scripts/reading-list.mjs`. Titles are alphabetical within each theme and flow down compact columns. The page supports title/author search (press `/`) and theme tabs with live counts, and the full catalogue remains readable without JavaScript. Collection entries may represent multiple volumes, so counts describe catalogue entries rather than individual books.

Do not include account details, private download URLs, or full download dates in the public data. The reading list combines verified download-history metadata with additional books from backup metadata and personal additions. Backup upload timestamps are not treated as reading dates. Undated books appear in a separate section. Titles that cannot be reliably identified are held out until clarified.

## Build locally

Node 20 or newer is sufficient. No dependency installation is needed.

```sh
npm run build
npm run dev
```

## GitHub Pages

This repository is intended to be named `Okyumi.github.io` under the `Okyumi` account. In **Settings → Pages → Build and deployment**, choose **GitHub Actions**. The included workflow builds and publishes the site on each push to `main`. Alternatively, publish the committed `docs/` folder directly from the main branch. Do not enable both publication methods at once.

## Imported writing

Posts were imported from Yumi's public `featured-blogs` repository and the current featured sections of omoriyumi.com. The most recent essay, “The Ships of Theseus Rebuilt at Different Rates,” was taken from the original website. The English versions are retained; duplicate Chinese translations are omitted, and the remaining Chinese/Japanese passages are translated into English. Repository-only navigation is replaced with website navigation. Content provenance is recorded in each article’s metadata.

The Markdown parser is Marked 17.0.5 (MIT), included in `scripts/vendor/` with its license. JetBrains Mono and Source Serif 4 are loaded from Google Fonts, Cabinet Grotesk (detour only) from Fontshare, and mathematical typesetting from the versioned KaTeX CDN.
