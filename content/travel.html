Youth and student discounts

Before buying a ticket, look for a youth or student rate. They are often different offers: one may depend on your age, another on proof of enrollment, nationality, or residence.

Cards worth checking

  • European Youth Card (EYCA) — discounts on transport, accommodation, museums, activities, and more across Europe. You do not have to be European: the central digital card is available to people under 31 whose place of residence has no local issuer. Local cards have their own terms and prices.
  • International Student Identity Card (ISIC) — an internationally recognized way to prove student status and access participating discounts. Full-time students can apply with current proof of enrollment; there is no upper age limit. Check whether your school already issues a co-branded card.
  • International Youth Travel Card (IYTC) — an alternative for non-students aged 30 or younger, with access to many similar offers. Card prices and the available benefits vary by country.

You may not need a card

  • Belgian trains have reduced fares for travelers under 26. Compare the fare for your route and any optional pass rather than assuming there is one fixed youth price.
  • Vatican Museums offer a student reduction through age 25, with proof of enrollment for the current year. Age alone is not enough.
  • UCPA offers outdoor and sports holidays, including selected deals for ages 18–25 and departures for ages 18–30. Compare what is included: instruction, equipment, accommodation, meals, and transport.
  • Scotland’s free bus scheme is for eligible residents under 22 with an entitlement card. It is not a general free-travel offer for visiting tourists.

Work while you travel

A working holiday can give you time to live somewhere, take temporary jobs, and explore between them. Many schemes are for ages 18–30, with some extending to 35. Your passport determines which routes are available, how long you can stay, and what work you can do.

  • Australia — check the Working Holiday or Work and Holiday route for your nationality. Seasonal farm work, hospitality, and tourism are options to investigate; the visa conditions determine which jobs and extensions are permitted.
  • New Zealand — usually ages 18–30, or up to 35 under some agreements. Most schemes allow a stay of up to 12 months, with longer options for certain nationalities. A good route to investigate if you want to combine temporary work with time outdoors.
  • Canada: International Experience Canada (IEC) — includes Working Holiday, Young Professionals, and International Co-op categories. The available categories, age limits, and places vary by nationality; not everyone qualifies for every category.
  • United Kingdom: Youth Mobility Scheme — a route to live and work in the UK, normally for up to two years. The age limit is 30 or 35 depending on nationality, and some applicants must enter a ballot.
  • Japan and South Korea — options for spending longer in the country and experiencing everyday life. Check the agreement for your nationality and the instructions from the relevant embassy.

Start with eligibility, then work backward from the application window. Allow time for documents, any required language evidence, and the funds needed to support yourself before you find work.

Study and exchange abroad

Travel can also mean living with a host family, attending a local school, learning a language, or doing research. The right program depends on your stage of education; there is no single “under 30” rule for all of them.

  • Erasmus+ student mobility — study abroad through your university’s partner institutions, with possible grant support and academic recognition. Begin with your international office. Separately, Erasmus+ Youth Exchanges are short group learning exchanges for ages 13–30, rather than university study or subsidized holidays.
  • Youth for Understanding (YFU) — school exchanges centered on living with a host family and joining a local community. Check the program offered by the organization in your country.
  • AFS Intercultural Programs — school exchanges, shorter cultural programs, and other opportunities to live and learn abroad. Ages, destinations, duration, and scholarship availability depend on the program.
  • Kennedy-Lugar Youth Exchange and Study (YES) — a U.S. school exchange for eligible high school students from participating countries. Follow your country’s application page for the current intake, selection process, and support.
  • CIEE — study-abroad and exchange options at several stages of education. Compare the program’s academic fit, costs, and available scholarships.
  • DAAD — scholarships and funding routes for study and research in Germany. Search by your academic level, country, and intended subject.
  • Boren Awards — funding for U.S. undergraduate and graduate students to pursue international and language study in regions relevant to U.S. interests. Read the award’s eligibility and obligations carefully before applying.

Journeys worth making time for

Some trips are easier to fit into a long break: a multi-day trek, a slow train journey, or a chain of islands connected by boats. These ideas are grouped by the kind of journey and what you need to plan for.

Mountains and high plateaus

Peru, Bolivia, Chile, and Argentina. Consider the Inca Trail, Bolivia’s Salar de Uyuni, Torres del Paine, and longer walks through Patagonia. Distances are substantial, and some routes reach high elevations; leave room for travel days and acclimatization.

Tibet, Qinghai, and the Sichuan–Tibet route. Ideas include the G318 journey, Everest Base Camp on the Tibetan side, and the Mount Kailash circuit. Plan around high elevations, strong sun, and the entry permits and access rules that apply to your passport.

Nepal and Bhutan. In Nepal, look into Everest Base Camp, Annapurna Base Camp, and Langtang. Bhutan offers guided Himalayan journeys. Trek lengths vary widely; allow time for the route, acclimatization, and weather rather than trying to fit every trek into the same schedule.

Being young or physically fit does not protect you from altitude illness. Build a gradual ascent into the itinerary; the CDC’s altitude guidance is a useful starting point.

Polar landscapes

Iceland, Greenland, Alaska, and Arctic Norway. Northern lights, glacier walks, camping, and expedition cruises offer very different ways to experience the north. Expect weather to shape the itinerary, and budget for transport, equipment, and the right local expertise. Expedition and icebreaker cruises require more time and planning.

The Galápagos Islands

Ecuador’s island wildlife. Look into the giant-tortoise reserves on Santa Cruz, volcanic walks on Isabela, and snorkeling around Kicker Rock. Sea lions, penguins, and other wildlife make the islands remarkable, but the route matters: not every itinerary visits the same habitats.

Getting there usually involves flying via mainland Ecuador, then choosing between island stays, boat transfers, and a live-aboard itinerary. Trips can combine walking, snorkeling, and time at sea, so consider how much boat travel suits you.

December–May is generally warmer, with calmer seas; June–November is cooler, with stronger winds and rougher water. Both have their attractions. The Galápagos Conservancy’s planning guide explains the seasons and practical arrangements.

Volcanoes and tropical landscapes

Indonesia: Bali, Lombok, and Java. Build a route around Bromo at sunrise, Ijen’s crater, and a trek on Rinjani. These journeys can involve overnight departures, long road transfers, and demanding climbs. Check current park access when choosing dates.

The Philippines, Papua New Guinea, and Timor-Leste. Consider island diving, rainforest walks, and locally hosted cultural visits. Connections can take time, and heat and humidity may make a short walk feel much longer.

Hawaiʻi’s Big Island. Combine volcanic landscapes with stargazing on Maunakea. For lava viewing, use the open viewpoints listed by Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park; the experience depends on volcanic activity and current access. Allow for driving distances and changes in elevation.

Waterfalls and canyons

Victoria Falls, Zambia and Zimbabwe. Walking viewpoints and scenic flights offer different perspectives. Devil’s Pool is a guided, seasonal activity from the Zambian side, available only when river conditions permit.

Iguazú Falls, Argentina and Brazil. Explore the walking circuits on both sides, and consider a boat excursion for a closer view. Factor border crossings, distances, and humid weather into the itinerary.

The Grand Canyon, Arizona. Sunrise viewpoints, longer hikes, an overnight stay below the rim, and scenic flights are different kinds of trip. Choose the route for the time and preparation you have; temperatures at the rim and inside the canyon can differ greatly.

Islands and slower connections

Sri Lanka. Combine the hill-country train between Kandy and Ella, Sigiriya’s Lion Rock, and the southern coast. Slow rail travel and transfers are part of the journey, so avoid treating every stop as a quick day trip.

Madagascar. The Avenue of the Baobabs and the limestone landscapes of Tsingy are ideas for a longer route. Road conditions and limited infrastructure can make travel between sights take much longer than the distance suggests.

Flores, Sumba, and Komodo. Consider Komodo’s wildlife, pink-sand beaches, and nights aboard a boat under the stars. Compare flight connections and boat itineraries before settling on a route.

Samoa, Tonga, Fiji, and Vanuatu. Think about volcanic landscapes, village visits, and diving. Flights between islands can be infrequent and supplies expensive, so a focused trip may be more rewarding than trying to cover the whole region.

Wildlife, deserts, and open landscapes

Kenya, Tanzania, and Namibia. Ideas include the Serengeti migration, Kilimanjaro, and the Namib Desert. These are very different itineraries: choose between wildlife viewing, a mountain trek, and a desert journey, then plan for that trip’s season and transport.

The Sahara, southern Morocco, and Jordan’s Wadi Rum. Desert camping, camel journeys, and stargazing can be combined with local guides and vehicle transfers. Plan for dust and large changes between daytime and nighttime temperatures.

Chile’s Atacama Desert. Consider stargazing, salt-flat landscapes, and walks in the Valley of the Moon. Dry conditions and temperature changes are part of the experience; some excursions also reach high elevations.

Longer routes through mountains and countryside

Mongolia’s Gobi and Altai regions. Allow for long overland days, limited infrastructure, and pronounced temperature changes.

Northern Laos and Sa Pa, Vietnam. Mountain roads, walks, and locally hosted stays offer a slower way to get to know a place. Leave time for the journey between communities.

Myanmar’s Shan State. Defer this idea for now: the UK’s current travel advice advises against all travel to northern Shan State and all but essential travel to its southern and eastern parts.

The Canadian Rockies. A longer road trip can combine hiking, camping, and time in the mountains. Give each stop enough time rather than turning the journey into a sequence of long drives.
